# Gift 10 — Research Trail

## Indian urban couples — intimacy, conflict, empathy, validation, support and fulfilled expectations
A study of 91 urban Indian couples reported that happier couples reported agreement, empathy, validation, support, and fulfilled expectations; intimacy was associated with marital happiness and conflict negatively with it. This is a specific sample, not a universal rule for all Indian couples.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19161585/

## Relationship standards and constructive handling of differences
Research with Chinese couples found that similarity in relationship standards and constructive handling of differences were related to marital satisfaction through perceived communication patterns. It is useful for the expectations section, while recognizing that the sample is not Indian.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24015705/

## Daily emotional closeness and positive communication
A 2026 daily-diary study of cohabiting romantic couples examined emotional closeness, positive affect, and perceived positive communication in relation to daily relationship satisfaction. This supports treating small daily relational experiences as meaningful research questions, without claiming causation from one study.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42541717/

## Communication and satisfaction over time
Longitudinal research found concurrent links between negative communication and relationship satisfaction, while cautioning against overly simple claims that communication skill alone predicts later satisfaction.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34027722/

## Relationship education
A randomized controlled study found small improvements in relationship satisfaction and communication from relationship education, while noting that the mechanisms are not fully understood.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26501497/

## Emotional and sexual intimacy
Research with 335 married couples examined communication, emotional intimacy, sexual satisfaction, and relationship satisfaction, supporting the idea that intimacy should not be reduced to communication alone.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24111536/

## Sexual communication
A meta-analysis of 93 studies found positive associations between sexual communication and relationship and sexual satisfaction; quality of communication showed stronger associations than frequency.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34968095/

## Culture and love
A thematic review discusses how culture affects how love and intimacy are expressed. It supports avoiding the assumption that one Western relationship script fits every couple.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37647358/

## Young relationships and intimacy in India
Research on young people in Pune described romantic partnership formation and emphasized that physical intimacy should be wanted, informed, and safe. It is included to keep the intimacy sections consent-aware.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17101433/

## Family and intimacy in urban India
Research on young women's relationships in urban India describes how family support, education/career expectations, and social norms can shape intimacy and relationship development.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30328774/
